The Case

Only what the file supports

What follows separates inspected documents from the family case record. The family’s position is stated as their position. It is not adopted here as a finding that the conviction is wrongful.

01Background

A May 18, 2005 defense letter describes Abbas Ahmed as a 19-year-old Pakistani man, interviewed at the Arlington County Detention Center with an interpreter. The letter reports his account of limited English and of fear during police questioning. Those are the psychologist’s report and Ahmed’s reported account, not immigration or school records.Footnote 2

The family case record adds that he immigrated from Pakistan in June 2002, had no prior criminal trouble, and became a U.S. citizen through his father. The same psychologist’s letter says Ahmed reported having been in the United States only two years at the time of the 2005 evaluation. Those two accounts are not the same, and this website does not choose between them. His published author's note in Unlocked (2025) says he arrived from Pakistan at 17. No immigration or school records are posted here, so the timing details remain as the two accounts give them.Footnote 3

02Charges / Proceedings

Letters and the laboratory certificate place the matter in Arlington County, Virginia, captioned as a Commonwealth prosecution of Abbas Ahmed. The certificate names suspect Abbas Javed Ahmed, police case 040711050, and a file stamp of the Arlington circuit court clerk on May 31, 2005.Footnote 1

The family record lists Arlington circuit numbers CR04-1165, CR04-1166, CR04-1167, and CR04-1168, and four charges: two counts of forcible sodomy, one count of object sexual penetration, and one count of aggravated sexual battery. It says the alleged events were on July 11, 2004, and that trial was in June 2005. The May 18 letter describes the charges more generally as sexual battery and related counts. The indictment is not on this website. The four charges themselves are confirmed by the sentencing orders summarized below; the indictment itself is not posted here. The CR04 case numbers remain as the family record gives them.Footnote 3

03Conviction / Outcome

Trial began June 7, 2005. Sentencing hearing was September 9, 2005, before Judge Joanne F. Alper, and orders entered September 22, 2005 — the legally operative date — imposed four consecutive terms: two counts forcible sodomy (8 years + 10 years), object sexual penetration (12 years), and aggravated sexual battery (5 years), 35 years total, none suspended, plus a $10,000 fine and $10,355 costs. Substitute orders entered February 1, 2006 vacated the September orders over an appellate-counsel listing error, and the Court of Appeals treated September 22 as final judgment and ruled the February vacatur untimely. The direct appeal ended on that timeliness ruling. State habeas was denied on February 12, 2009 (Final Order, CL08-1204). Federal habeas in the Eastern District of Virginia (No. 1:10-cv-184) was denied, and the Fourth Circuit appeal (No. 12-7831) ended without a certificate of appealability. Those outcomes are taken from the court orders and dockets in the case file; the opinions themselves are not posted here.Footnote 3

This website does not state that the conviction was wrongful. It states that the family is asking for review, and that the documents below are the materials actually supplied.

04Basis for the innocence claim

The family case record says, in substance, that the family believes Abbas Ahmed is innocent and wants an independent review. That is the family’s position. It is not an independent finding.Footnote 4

The November 10, 2004 laboratory certificate reports no evidence of seminal fluid or blood on items 1 through 4, no spermatozoa on the smears it lists, and no foreign DNA types on the penile swabs or on the perianal/buttocks swabs compared with the complainant’s profile. It also reports that no DNA result was obtained from the interior crotch of the underpants, and that items 5 through 11 were not analyzed after conversations with the assistant prosecutor. Absence of a foreign DNA profile is not proof that the charged acts did not occur.Footnote 1

Two letters from Marc J. Tabackman, Ph.D., give a defense opinion that the July 11, 2004 interrogation had features he associates with a “coerced-compliant” statement, and that he could not say the confession was false. A letter from Elisabeth Ridgely, a SANE nurse, gives a defense opinion that the medical findings she reviewed did not conclusively show a sexual assault. Neither letter is a court ruling. The graphic medical letter is not pictured.Footnote 5

05Current status

Direct appeal, state habeas, and federal habeas are all concluded, as the Case page summarizes from the court orders and dockets. No Virginia writ of actual innocence has ever been filed. The Mid-Atlantic Innocence Project reviewed the case and closed it on December 5, 2022 for lack of legally qualifying new evidence; its letter is posted in Documents.Footnote 3

Arlington County publishes a Conviction Review Unit that accepts claims of factual innocence and requests for post-conviction forensic testing. Its page was checked on October 16, 2026. This website does not say a petition has been submitted.Footnote 6

06What supporters are asking for — and how to support

The campaign's position is direct: Abbas Ahmed is innocent, and his freedom should be pursued through clemency, conviction review, and genuinely new evidence — in that order. That conclusion is advocacy, not a court exoneration, and this site does not claim a court has formally exonerated him.

Clemency: absolute pardon is the innocence track, officially a remedy of last resort, requiring exhaustion of all judicial remedies including a Writ of Actual Innocence — and no such writ has ever been filed in this case (checked against the case record). Conditional pardon is sentence modification and officially rare. Petitions go through the Secretary of the Commonwealth, the Parole Board investigates, a 2025 law compels law enforcement, courts, and the forensics lab to turn over unredacted files within 30 working days, there is no hearing and no appeal if denied, but refiling is allowed. Sex-offender-registry relief comes only from the circuit court of conviction, never the governor.Footnote 7

Conviction review: the Arlington County Conviction Review Unit, established by Commonwealth's Attorney Parisa Dehghani-Tafti, reviews factual-innocence claims, investigation and prosecution-integrity challenges, and post-conviction DNA-testing requests. Intake is an 11-page Screening Questionnaire to CWA_CRU@arlingtonva.us, for Arlington County or Falls Church felonies. Claims already litigated before the original jury may be declined, review is never guaranteed, and questionnaire content is voluntary and can be used in court — so it should go through an attorney before submitting. No application has been sent from this site.Footnote 6

Support that actually helps: share this page, write respectfully to the public review office (not to private individuals, and never naming the complainant), write to Abbas at Greensville Correctional Center (his address and the mail rules are on the Contact page; legal and privileged mail uses the separate distribution-center address given there), and follow the Freedom 4 Abbas family campaign once its pages are published. There is no donation button and no petition on this site yet, because no donation recipient and no petition were supplied — do not treat the contact form as a donation or petition.

Honesty note: this site accounts for adverse on-record facts rather than hiding them — the interrogation pages contain admissions, the child showered before the SANE exam (which explains null results), his counsel twice agreed on the record to proceed without an interpreter (so the broad 'did not understand English' argument is not used here), and the Commonwealth itself entered the DNA certificate into evidence at trial. 'No foreign DNA detected' on tested swabs is not, by itself, proof of innocence. That discipline is what makes the innocence case checkable, and therefore supportable.

What is established vs. what is disputed

The left column is reserved for court-record facts with a source. The right column holds disputes, arguments, and anything not yet sourced. Neither column is filled with a case allegation today.

Established from supplied case sources

  • A Virginia forensic certificate dated November 10, 2004, lab N04-7595, names Abbas Javed Ahmed and was file-stamped in Arlington Circuit Court on May 31, 2005.
  • That certificate reports no evidence of seminal fluid or blood on items 1 through 4, no spermatozoa on the smears it lists, no foreign DNA types on the penile swabs, and no analysis of items 5 through 11 after conversations with the assistant prosecutor. It also reports that no DNA result was obtained from the interior crotch of the underpants.
  • Defense letters dated April 15, April 18, and May 18, 2005 exist. Two are posted. The medical letter is summarized and not pictured. They are opinions to defense counsel.
  • The sentencing orders were entered on September 22, 2005 and impose four consecutive terms totaling 35 years, none suspended, with a $10,000 fine and $10,355 in costs.
  • State habeas was denied on February 12, 2009 (Final Order, CL08-1204), federal habeas (No. 1:10-cv-184) was denied, and no Virginia writ of actual innocence has ever been filed.
  • Defense counsel moved to suppress Abbas's statement; the motion was heard on May 26, 2005 and denied on June 22, 2005.

Disputed, argued, or not yet sourced

  • Whether Abbas Ahmed committed the charged acts. The family believes he did not. Nothing on this website is a finding of innocence or of guilt.
  • The charges and the 35-year sentence are established by the sentencing orders (summarized on this page; the orders themselves are not posted). The verdict forms and jury instructions are not in the posted file.
  • Whether the interrogation produced a false or legally involuntary statement. The defense psychologist says he cannot determine validity. The court denied the defense suppression motion on June 22, 2005; the grounds argued at the hearing are not in the available record.
  • Whether the medical findings support or fail to support a particular count. The defense consultant’s opinion is not the prosecution expert’s testimony, which is not in this file.
  • What the trial transcript shows about the interrogation's written-apology episode and the closing arguments. The transcript is held in the family's case file and has been reviewed in the legal research; it is not posted on this website.
