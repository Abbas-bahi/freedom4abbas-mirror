Why Review Is Needed

Questions first. Answers only with a source.

The headings below are organizational prompts from the site brief. They are not findings, and they are not a claim that a jury trial, a dispute, or new evidence has been established.

01

What did the laboratory test, and what did it leave untested?

Question grounded in the November 10, 2004 certificate. Not a claim that the results prove innocence.

The certificate reports no evidence of seminal fluid or blood on items 1 through 4, no spermatozoa on the listed smears, and no foreign DNA types on the penile swabs. It also reports no DNA result from the interior crotch of the underpants, and no analysis of items 5 through 11 after conversations with Assistant Commonwealth’s Attorney Molly Newton. A reviewer can ask what those choices mean. The certificate itself does not answer whether the charged acts occurred.

02

What do the defense psychology letters actually say about the interrogation?

Question about opinions in letters to counsel. Not a finding that police committed a crime.

Marc J. Tabackman, Ph.D., wrote on April 15 and May 18, 2005 that he saw features he associates with a coerced-compliant statement, and that he could not say the confession was false. The May letter also records Ahmed’s report that he feared being beaten. That is an account in a defense letter, not proof of an assault. Defense counsel moved to suppress the statement; after a hearing on May 26, 2005, the court denied the motion on June 22, 2005. The motion papers do not state the grounds argued, and the hearing transcript has not been located.

03

What medical opinion was supplied, and what are its limits?

Question about one defense letter. The prosecution examiner’s testimony is not in this file.

Elisabeth Ridgely’s April 18, 2005 letter, summarized without the graphic detail, tells defense counsel that the findings she reviewed did not, in her opinion, conclusively show a sexual assault. An injury does not automatically prove the charged act, and the absence of an injury she considered significant does not automatically disprove it. She was retained as a defense consultant and did not testify at trial; Dr. Tabackman testified, limited to general risk factors. The images of her letter are withheld because they describe a child.

04

What review route is identified, and what is still missing?

A procedural question. Not a promise that relief is available.

Arlington County’s Conviction Review Unit publicly accepts innocence claims and requests under the Virginia forensic-testing statute. The family record says ordinary appeals and habeas are exhausted and that an innocence writ has not been filed. The procedural history is documented from the court orders and dockets (see the Case page). Still not posted: the appellate opinions themselves, and a current chain of custody for the evidence in lab N04-7595.
