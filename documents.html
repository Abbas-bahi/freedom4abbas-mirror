    10 November 2004 · Laboratory certificate

    A two-page Virginia laboratory certificate. The complainant’s name is covered on the public copies; everything else, including the forensic scientist’s signature, is shown as it appears. The reading text summarizes the reported results and does not treat them as proof of innocence.

    Readable text / summary

    Commonwealth of Virginia, Division of Forensic Science. Certificate of Analysis dated November 10, 2004. FS Lab No. N04-7595. Police case 040711050. Addressed to D. L. Guenther, Arlington County Police Department. A file stamp reads May 31, 2005, Circuit Court, Arlington County, David A. Bell, Clerk. Suspect listed: Ahmed, Abbas Javed. The complainant’s name is redacted on this site. Evidence received August 16, 2004, and a buccal sample received October 19, 2004. Items 1, 2, 3, and 4: the certificate states that no evidence of seminal fluid or blood was detected on these items.     Letter of Marc J. Tabackman, Ph.D., on the interrogation audiotapes

    15 April 2005 · Defense psychology letter

    A defense letter about the July 11, 2004 interrogation. It is an expert opinion, not a court finding. The writer says he cannot determine with certainty whether the confession was valid.

    Readable text / summary

    Greenside Psychological Associates. Marc J. Tabackman, Ph.D., Forensic Psychology. Letter dated April 15, 2005, to Denman A. Rucker, Esq., re Commonwealth of Virginia v. Abbas Ahmed. He writes that he reviewed audiotapes of a police interrogation dated July 11, 2004. He states it is not possible to determine with certainty whether the confession was valid. He says he identified elements that, in his view, can increase the likelihood of a false confession when used with a youthful, anxious subject: a persuasive rather than neutral stance, repetition, minimization, praise and promises of help, an attempt to obtain a written apology, and what he calls possible deception about DNA evidence. He describes the setting as not videotaped, late, and lasting about two to three hours, with a female officer present and then a male officer. He describes Ahmed, from the tapes and his clinical impression, as young, with limited English, naive, and passive. He writes that it is questionable whether Ahmed fully understood the Miranda rights. His summary calls the statement a “coerced-compliant” confession of “highly suspect validity,” possibly based on a belief that the truth would come out later, and says a more definite opinion would require an in-depth evaluation. That is his opinion. It is not a ruling that the statement was illegally obtained or untrue.

    Letter of Marc J. Tabackman, Ph.D., after a clinical interview

    18 May 2005 · Defense psychology letter

    A second defense letter, after an April 29, 2005 interview at the jail with an interpreter. Opinions and Ahmed’s reported account are labeled as such.

    Readable text / summary

    Letter dated May 18, 2005, from Marc J. Tabackman, Ph.D., to Denman A. Rucker. He writes that he interviewed Abbas Ahmed on April 29, 2005, for about three hours at the Arlington County Detention Center, with an interpreter described in the letter as fluent in Ahmed’s native language, and reviewed discovery including the July 11, 2004 interrogation audiotapes. He describes Ahmed as a 19-year-old Pakistani man, charged in Arlington County with sexual battery and related counts. He reports screening that placed English reading recognition at a 5th-grade level and verbal comprehension at a level equivalent to a six-year-old. Those are the expert’s reported screening interpretations, not school transcripts or an independent assessment by this website. He reports Ahmed’s account of the interrogation, including a claim that Ahmed did not fully understand the waiver, feared police would beat him, and believed he would be helped if he said what was wanted. Those sentences are what the letter says Ahmed reported. They are not a finding that an officer assaulted him. The letter’s conclusion says that, in the writer’s opinion to a reasonable degree of psychological certainty, many aspects of the interrogation were coercive, confusing, intimidating, and/or threatening, and that Ahmed likely produced a “coerced-compliant” confession. The same letter says it is not possible to assess the true validity of the statements to police.

    Mid-Atlantic Innocence Project closing letter

    5 December 2022 · Innocence project letter

    A two-page closing letter from Jay Millikan, Staff Attorney, Mid-Atlantic Innocence Project. It says MAIP believed in Abbas's innocence and hired a Virginia contract investigator specifically for his case, but could not litigate without legally qualifying “new evidence.” The page images are posted below in full. The verified reading text is also given, including the limiting point.

    Readable text / summary

    From: Jay Millikan, Staff Attorney, Mid-Atlantic Innocence Project, 1413 K Street NW, Suite 1100, Washington, DC 20005. To: Abbas Ahmed #1182708. Date: December 5, 2022. Closure: after thorough consideration by Millikan and other MAIP staff, MAIP "will be unable to move forward on litigating your case." Expressly not a disbelief finding: "this was not a decision we made because we don't believe in your innocence… we hired a contract investigator in Virginia specifically to look into your case, and your case only, because we believed in your innocence. We continue to believe in your innocence." The legal barrier: Virginia law requires MAIP to prove innocence through "new evidence" — evidence uncovered since trial that was not known, or could not possibly have been known, at the time of trial. The opportunity theory: MAIP's investigator interviewed the family members present in the apartment and confirmed the theory that Abbas would have had an incredibly difficult time committing the offense given the number of people in the small apartment — almost no opportunity. BUT: "this is an issue that your attorney did bring up and argue at trial, and therefore it does not count as 'new evidence' under the Virginia statute." That limiting point weakens any claim that the opportunity argument was never presented at trial, and it is included here deliberately. Door left open: "if new evidence does ever emerge that might suggest your innocence, of course your case could potentially be re-opened at MAIP." MAIP returned Abbas's documents to him. This letter is a personal-belief and procedural statement by an innocence project, not a court finding of innocence. The letter itself is shown below, exactly as received.

    Letter of Elisabeth Ridgely, SANE, commenting on the medical report

    18 April 2005 · Defense medical-comment letter

    A defense consultant’s letter about the prosecution medical examination. The page images are not posted. They discuss a child’s body in detail. A short, non-graphic summary is given instead.

    Images withheld. The reading text is a non-graphic summary only.

    Readable text / summary

    The supplied images are a faxed letter dated April 18, 2005, on the letterhead of Elisabeth Ridgely, BS, RN, LNCC, SANE, to Denman Rucker, re Commonwealth of Virginia v. Abbas Ahmed. This website is not posting those images. They describe a child’s medical examination in graphic terms. The family case record uses a slightly different spelling of the consultant’s name. This site has not reconciled the spellings against another record. Non-graphic summary of her comments: she writes that she dissected the examination report; that she considered the reported timing and the child’s presentation; that non-genital trauma was recorded as none; and that, in her opinion, the genital findings she discussed did not conclusively establish a sexual assault and could have other causes, including irritation or a small injury. She also writes that she did not see sperm noted. This is her opinion to defense counsel. It is not a trial ruling, and it does not by itself prove innocence. The family record says she was not called as a witness. That point is not confirmed from a transcript on this website. The examination report itself is not published — it is a child’s medical file. Separately, Elisabeth Ridgely, a SANE-credentialed nurse and legal nurse consultant, reviewed that report section by section for the defense (letter to defense counsel, April 18, 2005) and questioned whether the findings fit the allegation. Her letter is attorney correspondence, so it is summarized here rather than published.
